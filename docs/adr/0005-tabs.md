# ADR-5: Tabs

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-21). The plan is `docs/ROADMAP.md`, Sprint 10.

## Context

The shell shows one page at a time, and leaving it destroys it (invariant 3):
the app's `Store` and linear memory are dropped, and coming back starts it
afresh. That is the sandbox working, and it also means a person cannot keep
the rates open while checking a list, or go back to a half-filled form in
another app. Every browser solved this with tabs, and people expect them.

What tabs must not do is undo what invariant 3 buys: an app that is not on
screen may not keep running, keep the CPU, keep the network, or keep memory
without bound. Nor may an app reach the tabs — they are chrome (invariant 2).

## Decision

**A tab is a place a page is shown**, with its own page, its own Back and
Forward, and its own status line. One tab is shown; the others are **parked**.

- **A parked tab is frozen.** Its app is kept — its `Store`, its memory, its
  element tree — but it is not called: no `update`, no frames, its
  `request-frame` waits. Nothing it asked for is lost: a fetch already sent
  finishes on the host and waits for the app's `fetch-poll` when it is shown
  again. A parked **web page** keeps its address only: its webview is
  destroyed and made again when the tab is shown, because a hidden webview
  would keep running the page's scripts, timers and audio.
- **Switching is not leaving.** Invariant 3 is restated: *leaving an app —
  navigating its tab elsewhere, or closing the tab — destroys it*. Showing
  another tab parks it.
- **Bounded.** At most `MAX_TABS` (8) tabs. Parked tabs together may keep
  `PARKED_MEMORY` (512 MiB) of guest memory; past that, the parked tab shown
  least recently is **discarded**: its app is dropped and only its address and
  title are kept, and shown again it opens afresh — as a browser discards a
  tab under memory pressure. The tab being shown is never discarded. The
  per-app limits are unchanged.
- **The chrome's alone.** A strip above the address bar: each tab's title, a
  close button, and `+` for a new tab on the start page. On the desktop,
  Ctrl+T, Ctrl+W and Ctrl+Tab too. A guest's `open-app` navigates its own
  tab; nothing in the WIT opens, switches or closes one.
- **The same on a phone** (invariant 8): the strip scrolls sideways; Back
  goes back within the shown tab, and from its start page leaves the app as
  before.
- **Testable headlessly**: `--new-tab`, `--tab` and `--close-tab` by frame,
  and each frame prints the strip (`[tabs] 1 store | *2 counter`).

## Consequences

- `Shell` stays one tab's worth of state; `tabs::Tabs` holds the parked ones
  and swaps them with the shown one, so everything that drives "the shell"
  drives the shown tab unchanged. The window keeps a little state per tab
  (its widgets' buffers, the tree's textures) and swaps it with the tab.
- A registry refresh reaches every tab, parked or not.
- Memory is now bounded per app *and* for the parked tabs together; the
  worst case is the shown app's 512 MiB plus `PARKED_MEMORY`.
